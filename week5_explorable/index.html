Caught in the Web

With great vocabulary comes great responsibility

Spin the web

The right side of the web holds the words that come after your word, the left side the words that come before it. Thicker threads mean more frequent n-grams. Click any word on the web to read the real sentences behind that thread.

How it works

All 303 Marvel Wikipedia pages from the course data are stored inside this page. When you type a word, your browser finds every place it occurs and looks at its neighbours: the word just before and just after it, and then two and three steps away. Every time you flip a switch, the text is cut into words again and a new web is spun.

The details, and what to be careful with

  • What counts as a word: the text is cut into pieces (tokens) at every space and every punctuation mark. A comma or a bracket becomes a piece of its own, and so does the ending 's. So Death's Head becomes three pieces: death 's head.
  • Hyphens: a hyphen also cuts a word in two, so Spider-Woman becomes spider - woman. Turn on keep hyphenated words whole to keep it as one word: spider-woman.
  • One page at a time: a thread never jumps from one page to the next. All the words in an n-gram come from the same page.
  • Only the biggest threads are drawn: the threads slider decides how many branches each word gets. The smaller ones are still counted, just not drawn.
  • Left and right are counted separately: a word on the left and a word on the right don't always appear in the same sentence. Click one on each side to see where they really meet. Words that never appear with your pick fade out.
  • Thickness: the thicker the thread, the more common the n-gram. Thickness grows with the square root of the count, so rare threads stay visible next to very common ones.
  • Removing stopwords: small words like the and of are deleted before counting. So two words that had a small word between them can end up side by side as a bigram.
